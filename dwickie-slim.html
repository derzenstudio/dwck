Six skills. One pair of hands.

People keep asking “so what do you actually do?” — this is the honest list. Each one is a real, separate craft I trained on purpose, not a keyword added to impress an algorithm.

See them in the pictures

  • Character design

    Faces, casts, costumes, expressions — personalities you can draw twice and still recognise.

  • Concept art

    Worlds before stories: environments, props and moods built to be lived in by someone else's plot.

  • Poster & editorial

    Single-image arguments. Layout, hierarchy and one metaphor strong enough to carry a whole page.

  • Digital painting

    Light, atmosphere, texture. Painted scenes large enough to walk up close to and check my nerve.

  • Traditional ink

    Nibs, brushes, India ink. No undo button — the oldest discipline here and the one that keeps the others honest.

  • Animation & loops

    Frame-by-frame patience plus After Effects choreography, for drawings that insist on moving.

How a piece gets made, honestly

No mystique, no genius act. Just five repeatable steps that turn a passing thought into something you'd hang on a wall. When you ask me for one, this is exactly the road your idea travels.

Start step one with me

  1. Idea

    A sentence, scribbled badly

    Everything begins as twenty words in the notes app, usually written at an unreasonable hour. If the sentence survives a week, it earns a drawing.

  2. Sketch

    Ugly thumbnails, many of them

    Twenty fast versions before lunch. The good idea hides among the bad ones — I just keep drawing until it stops hiding.

  3. Line

    Ink decides everything

    Final linework, permanent and unapologetic. Once the nib touches paper there is no undo, only commitment — which is why the lines look the way they do.

  4. Color

    Light does the arguing

    Flat colours first, then dozens of careful layers of glow and shadow until the image has weather. A painting should have a climate, not a palette.

  5. Send

    It leaves the desk

    Prints packed by hand, files named properly, originals shipped anywhere the world bothers to mail to. Then back to step one, because the next obsession is already scratching at the door.

Good to know before you ask

Anything else? Write to dwickie@example.com and I'll answer between brushstrokes — usually within a day or two.

In waves. I open a few slots when the current batch ends, because half-finished work haunts me otherwise. The fastest way to know is to simply write — I keep a yes/no list and update it honestly.

Almost. There is a short list of things I won't draw — hate imagery, tobacco, gambling, political propaganda — and a longer list of things I probably shouldn't attempt, like realistic horses. Everything in between, bring it. Weird beats safe every time.

Because a poster and a twenty-character cast are different animals, and pretending otherwise helps nobody. Tell me the size, the deadline and the dream; I'll quote a number the same week, and if it's beyond you we part as friends.

Some. The pieces I release as prints are marked in the gallery captions' spirit — basically, if I could bear to let the original leave, it becomes a print. Archives ship worldwide from Yogyakarta, rolled, never folded.

Gently: no. You're hiring twelve years of my hand, not a photocopy of someone else's. What I will do is listen hard to what you love about that style — the warmth, the chaos, the line weight — and give you my version of it. That's the interesting trade.
